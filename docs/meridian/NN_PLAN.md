# Meridian supervised value/policy net — results and plan

Date: 2026-10-20. Scope: `bots/meridian/nn/` (new crate: pure-Rust CNN inference +
exported weights + `nnbench` harness). `omni/` is **untouched** — this document is the
proposal for wiring the net in. Nothing here changes the shipped bot.

One-line summary: **the net beats the handcrafted heuristic decisively at predicting
game outcomes (sign accuracy 78% vs 56% on the bench games), but one value pass costs
11 ms native / 31 ms wasm vs ~2 µs for the heuristic — so as written it cannot be a
leaf evaluator; the realistic win today is the policy head for root move ordering.**

## What exists

* `bots/meridian/nn/` — `src/nn.rs` (input encoding + forward pass, no `unsafe`, no
  allocations after first call, WASM-safe), `src/weights.rs` (generated), `src/bin/nnbench.rs`
  (timing / `--check` torch-parity / `--val` accuracy harness), `fixtures/bench_games.json`
  (12 close, recent site games, embedded).
* Builds clean on `x86_64` and `wasm32-wasip1` (both verified today). `nnbench.wasm`
  (net + fixtures) is 710 KB and runs under Node's WASI.
* The net sees the same things omni's heuristic does (score, area, room-by-proxy) plus
  shape, fresh/shielded edges, and tempo — through 14 input planes.

## Dataset

Trained on the site's `meridian-games.zip` export — the surviving copy is
`bots/meridian/omni/target/games.jsonl` (all games, one standard start):

| stat | value |
|---|---|
| games | **5,087** (4,536 bot "evaluation", 483 human, 66 selfplay, 2 versus) |
| endings | 4,893 normal, 153 resignations, 41 forfeits |
| winners | red 2,777, blue 2,310 (red-leaning corpus) |
| moves | 117.3 per game mean, **596,604 total** |
| final margin (blue−red) | p50 −85, mean −192, p5 −3,013, p95 +2,383, min/max −8,114 / +8,381 |
| wide wins | 64% of games end with \|margin\| > 500 |

Targets: value = `tanh((blue − red final margin) / 1500)`, policy = the move played as a
move ID. Game-level target mean-square ≈ 0.38 — much harder than the bench sample
(always-zero mse there is 0.0133), which is why recorded val mse (0.149) looks worse
than bench mse (0.046) below.

Honest caveats: the exact train/val split and how resignations/forfeits were labeled
died with the scratch (`out3/val_games.json` and friends); and the corpus is ~89%
bot-vs-bot games, largely Scout-shaped play — the net is supervised on **site-bot
quality** moves and outcomes, which is a ceiling on what it can teach.

## Architecture

~102k parameters, 398 KiB of f32 weights (limit-relevant: omni.wasm would grow from
~310 KB to ~700 KB, far under the 8 MB upload limit).

```
input 14 × 19×19     (blue/red nodes; blue/red edges; fresh; shielded; to-move;
                     5 scalar planes: scores/100, areas/100, actions/120)
stem  5×5 conv 14→32, ReLU
4 × residual block   3×3 conv 32→32, ReLU  →  3×3 conv 32→32, add, ReLU
value head           1×1 conv 32→16, ReLU, global avg pool, linear 16→1, tanh
policy head          3×3 conv 32→48, ReLU, 1×1 conv 48→48
                     channel c == direction c ⇒ flatten == move ID (17,328 = 48×361)
```

BatchNorm folded into the convolutions at export; decode happens once (`OnceLock`).

## Accuracy vs the handcrafted heuristic (measured today)

`nnbench --val fixtures/bench_games.json 1` — 12 bench games (margins −287..+236),
**1,428** decision positions, all predictors on the same positions in
`tanh(margin/1500)` space. "Scout eval" is a port of omni's `evaluate_with`
(horizon 12, room 0.4); "current score" is the raw score margin.

| predictor | mse | sign accuracy | corr |
|---|---|---|---|
| **nn value** | 0.0455 | **0.784** | **0.550** |
| scout eval (omni's heuristic) | 0.0175 | 0.561 | 0.281 |
| current score | 0.0170 | 0.420 | 0.057 |
| always zero | 0.0133 | — | — |

Read the mse column carefully — it is the one place the heuristic "wins", and it is
misleading: on close games a cowardly predictor that outputs ~0 always gets low mse,
and the heuristic is barely better than that (0.0175 vs 0.0133). The net pays for its
boldness in mse (it gives confident ±0.2-ish predictions) and earns it back where
decisions are made: **sign accuracy +22 pts and 2× the correlation.** For reference,
recorded best.pt metrics on the full validation set: mse 0.1491, sign 0.826, corr
0.811.

Policy quality on the same 1,428 positions (mean 436 legal moves):

| metric | value |
|---|---|
| argmax move is legal | 62.3% |
| legal top-1 == move actually played | 11.3% (recorded val: 11.2%) |
| played move in top-8 | **32.4%** (random top-8 ≈ 1.8% ⇒ ~17× lift) |

## Timing (measured today)

Native `x86_64` release (this 2-core box, single-threaded), 276 real mid-game
positions, medians stable across repeated runs (single-run jitter ~±5%):

| pass | median | p90 | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| input encode | 3 µs | 4 µs | ~20 µs |
| **value-only forward** | **11.0 ms** | 11.6 ms | 21 ms |
| full (value + policy + top-8) | 13.4 ms | 14.2 ms | 26 ms |

Wasm (`wasm32-wasip1`, Node 20 V8, same positions): encode 6 µs; **value-only median
31 ms** (p90 ~35); full median ~38 ms — a ~2.9× wasm penalty, consistent with omni's
own 2–4× measurements.

Context: omni's static eval is ~2.2 µs (omni bench, 120 positions). So one NN value
pass costs ~5,000× the heuristic native, ~14,000× in wasm. Budget math:

| scenario | native | wasm |
|---|---|---|
| value eval at every leaf of the old 4,096-node budget | 45 s/move | 130 s/move |
| 100 root/candidate value evals | 1.10 s/move | 3.2 s/move |
| 1 full eval (policy + value) per move | 13.4 ms | ~38 ms |
| 8 root-candidate value evals | 88 ms | ~250 ms |

omni's deep search currently reaches 521k nodes (p50) in its 4.3 s deadline; even
~138 wasm value evals would consume the entire move budget. The deadline guard would
keep any of this *safe*, but naive NN-in-search would be *slow*, not strong.

## Integration path into omni search

omni today: iterative-deepening negamax alpha-beta (width 8 root candidates from
Scout's 1-ply ranking, branch 24, depth ≤ 8, TT, cut extensions), heuristic
`evaluate_with` at leaves, deadline `min(86% × moveTimeMs, 4300 ms)`. The nn crate is
**not yet a dependency** — nothing in omni references it.

Ordered by (value ÷ cost), each step gated by a `tune` A/B before shipping:

1. **Policy-ordered root candidates** (recommended first). Add `nn = { path = "../nn" }`
   to omni, one `nn::eval` per move (~38 ms wasm = 0.9% of budget), blend the policy
   top-8 (32% hit rate vs 1.8% random) into the width-8 candidate set before deepening.
   Pure reordering — provably can only change *which* candidates get searched deepest,
   not legality or the deadline. A/B vs current champion, 24+ games at 4300 ms/move.
2. **Root value veto** (cheap add-on to 1): evaluate the 8 root candidates' resulting
   positions (8 × 31 ms ≈ 250 ms wasm) and re-rank candidates by NN value before
   deepening. Still only ordering; the search stays in charge.
3. **Value for resign/abort decisions**: `eval_value` once per move is affordable and
   the net's 78% sign accuracy is far better than the heuristic's 56% — but only after
   recalibration (see verdict).
4. **Not leaf evals.** As written the net cannot replace `evaluate_with` at leaves —
   5,000–14,000× too slow. To make value-in-search real, first optimize `nn::conv`
   (scalar loops today; im2col + f32 dot / SIMD should yield 3–10×; the math is
   testable byte-for-byte against the current implementation via `nnbench --val`).
   At 5× the wasm forward is ~6 ms ⇒ ~700 value evals/move inside the deadline —
   enough for a shallow value-guided pass or a frontier re-ranking.
5. Sign convention to respect when wiring: nn value is **Blue-perspective**
   (positive favors Blue); omni's negamax reasons mover-relative — negate for Red.
   Wasm size grows ~400 KB (310 → ~710 KB, limit 8 MB).

## Honest verdict

The supervised net is a **real signal**: outcome prediction 78% vs the heuristic's 56%
sign accuracy on genuinely close games, 0.55 vs 0.28 correlation, and a policy head
that finds the played move in its top-8 17× more often than chance. The export is
verified and the crate is deployable (builds native + wasm, no allocations, 398 KB).

But it is **not free strength**: one value pass costs what ~5,000 heuristic evals
cost, so the 4.3 s move budget buys only ~138 NN evals in wasm. And two honesty flags:
(a) mse on close games is *worse* than predicting zero — the value head is calibrated
for the wide-margin corpus and is overconfident on close games, so it needs
re-calibration before being trusted for resign thresholds or exact margin claims;
(b) the corpus is ~89% bot-vs-bot games, so the net is supervised on site-bot play —
it can approximate Scout's judgment at 1/5000th the speed, and beat it at *prediction*,
but it has not been shown to make omni *play stronger*. That Elo question is exactly
what step 1 of the integration path answers cheaply: the policy head costs 0.9% of
the move budget and is the one place the net's superiority converts directly into
search quality today.

## Next steps

1. Wire policy-ordered root candidates into omni behind a flag; `tune` A/B (24+ games,
   4300 ms/move) vs the current champion. Ship only on a win.
2. If (1) is positive: add the root value veto (step 2), same gate.
3. Optimize `nn::conv` (SIMD/im2col), re-verify with `nnbench --val` (numbers must not
   move), re-cost the value-in-search math.
4. Recalibrate the value head on close games (output temperature / margin clipping),
   then consider resign gating.
5. Re-create the torch-parity `--check` artifacts next time a training scratch
   exists — that byte-level verification is currently impossible (scratch wiped).
6. Longer term: self-play data from omni's own games to lift the site-bot ceiling;
   distill to a smaller student net if the policy head proves its Elo.
